Introduction to System Security

System and data security refers to the protection of computer systems, networks, and data from unauthorized access, damage, or theft. In today's digital world, where information is a valuable asset, security has become essential for individuals, businesses, and governments.

What is Computer Security?

Computer security is the protection of computer systems and information from harm, theft, and unauthorized use. It involves three key principles known as the CIA Triad:

Confidentiality

Information is accessible only to authorized users

Integrity

Information is accurate and complete

Availability

Information is accessible when needed

Why Security Matters

Financial LossTheft of money, fraud, recovery costs, fines for data breaches
Reputation DamageLoss of customer trust, negative publicity, brand damage

Operational DisruptionSystem downtime, loss of productivity, data loss

Security Threats and Malware

Computer security risks are events or actions that could cause loss or damage to computer systems, data, or information. Understanding these risks is the first step in protecting against them.

What is Malware?

Malware (malicious software) is any software intentionally designed to cause damage to a computer, server, or network. Malware comes in many forms, each with different characteristics.

Types of Malware

Virus

Attaches to clean files and spreads

Worm

Self-replicates without user action

Trojan Horse

Disguised as legitimate software

Ransomware

Encrypts files, demands payment

Spyware

Secretly monitors user activity

Adware

Displays unwanted advertisements

Other Security Threats

Threat TypeDescriptionHow to Recognize
PhishingFraudulent attempts to obtain sensitive information by disguising as trustworthy entitiesUrgent language, suspicious links, spelling errors, fake sender address
Social EngineeringManipulating people into breaking security proceduresSomeone asking for passwords, pretending to be IT support
Denial of Service (DoS)Overwhelming a system to make it unavailableWebsite extremely slow or completely down
Man-in-the-MiddleIntercepting communication between two partiesUnsecured public Wi-Fi, suspicious certificate warnings
Insider ThreatsEmployees or associates who misuse their accessUnauthorized data access, copying sensitive information

Securing Your Computer System

Protecting computer systems requires a multi-layered approach. No single security measure is sufficient on its own. This is called Defense in Depth.

1. Strong Passwords

Password Strength Checker (Demo)

Enter a password to see strength

Password Best Practices

  • Use at least 12 characters (longer is better)
  • Include uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols
  • Don't use personal information (name, birthday, pet name)
  • Don't reuse passwords across different accounts
  • Use a password manager to generate and store complex passwords
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) whenever possible

2. Antivirus and Anti-malware Software

Essential Security Software

  • Antivirus: Detects and removes viruses, worms, trojans
  • Anti-malware: Specialized protection against spyware, adware, ransomware
  • Firewall: Monitors and controls network traffic
  • Keep software updated: Regular updates patch security vulnerabilities

3. Firewall Protection

What is a Firewall?

A firewall is a network security system that monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic based on predetermined security rules. It acts as a barrier between your trusted internal network and untrusted external networks like the Internet.

  • Hardware Firewall: Physical device protecting entire network
  • Software Firewall: Program protecting individual computer
  • Windows includes a built-in firewall (Windows Defender Firewall)
  • Routers also have built-in firewall capabilities

4. Regular Updates

Why Updates Matter

  • Security patches fix known vulnerabilities
  • Updates often include critical security fixes for zero-day exploits
  • Enable automatic updates for your operating system and software
  • Don't ignore update notifications - they're important for your security

Phishing and Social Engineering

Phishing is one of the most common and dangerous security threats. Attackers trick you into revealing sensitive information by pretending to be a legitimate organization.

How to Spot a Phishing Email

  • Urgent or threatening language - "Your account will be closed immediately!"
  • Suspicious links - Hover over links to see the real destination (don't click!)
  • Spelling and grammar mistakes - Professional companies proofread their emails
  • Generic greetings - "Dear Customer" instead of your name
  • Unexpected attachments - Especially .exe, .zip, .scr files
  • Requests for personal information - Legitimate companies don't ask for passwords via email
  • Spoofed email address - Looks similar but has small differences (arnazon.com vs amazon.com)

Example Phishing Email

Subject: URGENT: Your Account Will Be Suspended

From: security@your-bank-security.com

Message: Dear Customer, We have detected unusual activity on your account. To prevent suspension, please verify your information immediately by clicking http://bank-verify-account.com/login. Failure to do so within 24 hours will result in account suspension.

⚠️ RED FLAGS: Suspicious sender address, urgent language, generic greeting, fake link

What to Do If You Receive a Phishing Email

  • DO NOT click any links or download attachments
  • DO NOT reply or provide any information
  • Report it - Forward to the company being impersonated
  • Delete the email - Then empty your trash folder
  • If you accidentally clicked a link, run a security scan immediately

Data Backup and Recovery

The 3-2-1 Backup Rule

  • 3 - Keep at least 3 copies of your data
  • 2 - Store copies on 2 different media types
  • 1 - Keep 1 copy off-site (cloud or different location)

Backup Methods

MethodBest ForProsCons
External Hard DriveFull system backupsFast, large capacity, one-time costCan fail, susceptible to theft/damage
USB Flash DriveImportant documentsPortable, inexpensiveSmall capacity, easy to lose
Cloud StorageAutomatic backup, access anywhereOff-site, automatic, accessible from anywhereRequires internet, monthly cost for large storage
NAS (Network Attached Storage)Home/office networksCentralized, multiple users, RAID protectionExpensive, requires setup
Optical Discs (CD/DVD/Blu-ray)Archival storageLong-lasting if stored properlySlow, limited capacity

What to Back Up

  • Documents - School work, projects, important files
  • Photos and videos - Personal memories, irreplaceable
  • Contacts and calendars - Personal and professional information
  • Browser bookmarks - Saved websites and resources
  • Application settings - Custom configurations
  • System image - Complete backup of your operating system

Ethical Practices in ICT

Technology ethics involves making morally sound decisions when using ICTs. Ethical practices go beyond following laws to consider the impact of our actions on others and society.

Core Principles of Digital Ethics

  • Respect for Privacy - Protect personal information and respect others' privacy settings
  • Honesty and Integrity - Be truthful online, don't spread misinformation
  • Responsibility - Consider consequences of online actions
  • Respect for Property - Respect intellectual property rights (copyright, software licenses)
  • Non-Maleficence - Do no harm with technology
  • Beneficence - Use technology to help others and improve society

Intellectual Property Rights

Types of Intellectual Property

  • Copyright: Protects original creative works (books, music, software, photos)
  • Patents: Protects inventions and processes
  • Trademarks: Protects brand identifiers (logos, names, slogans)
  • Trade Secrets: Protects confidential business information

Ethical Use of Digital Content

  • Use licensed software and pay for subscriptions
  • Cite sources when using others' work
  • Use open-source or Creative Commons materials when appropriate
  • Don't share copyrighted materials without permission
  • Understand fair use provisions for education and research

Personal Security Checklist

Daily/Weekly Security Habits

  • Use strong, unique passwords for all accounts
  • Keep antivirus software running and updated
  • Install operating system and software updates
  • Back up important files regularly
  • Be suspicious of unexpected emails and links
  • Avoid using public Wi-Fi for sensitive activities
  • Lock your devices when not in use
  • Only download software from official sources
  • Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) when available
  • Review account activity regularly for suspicious access
