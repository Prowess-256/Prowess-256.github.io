Health and Safety in ICT

Understanding health risks and safety measures for prolonged computer and ICT device use

Ergonomics RSI Eye Strain Posture Workplace Safety Ergonomics

Learning Outcomes

  • Analyze common health problems associated with prolonged use of computers
  • Explain possible ways to avoid each health problem associated with ICT device use
  • Set up an ergonomic workspace to prevent injuries
  • Perform exercises and stretches to reduce strain
  • Communicate information on health and safety measures for computer use

Introduction to Health and Safety in ICT

Increased use of ICT today has greatly improved the quality of human life. However, it is important to note that if not carefully used, ICT can bring health hazards to human beings in a number of different ways. This is similar to a motor vehicle that eases transportation, but can cause accidents and even loss of lives when driven carelessly.

Key Concept: Health and Safety in ICT

Health and safety in ICT refers to the practices and measures taken to protect users from potential health risks associated with prolonged use of computers and other ICT devices. Just as we take precautions when using other tools, we must also be mindful of how we use technology to maintain our wellbeing.

Health Problems from Prolonged Computer Use

People spend several hours a day in front of a computer without thinking about the impact on their bodies. They physically strain themselves daily by extending their wrists, slouching, sitting without foot support, and straining to look at poorly placed monitors.

Understanding Cumulative Trauma Disorders

These practices can lead to cumulative trauma disorders or repetitive stress injuries (RSI), which create a life-long impact on health. Symptoms may include pain, muscle fatigue, loss of sensation, tingling, and reduced performance.

Common Health Problems

Computer Vision Syndrome (CVS)

Eye and vision problems from prolonged computer use

Symptoms: Dry eyes, blurred vision, headaches, eye strain

Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI)

Damage to muscles, tendons, and nerves from repetitive motions

Symptoms: Pain, numbness, tingling, weakness in hands/wrists

Musculoskeletal Disorders

Problems affecting muscles, bones, and joints

Symptoms: Back pain, neck pain, shoulder pain

Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

Pressure on the median nerve in the wrist

Symptoms: Numbness, tingling, pain in hand and fingers

Mental Fatigue & Stress

Mental exhaustion from prolonged concentration

Symptoms: Headaches, irritability, difficulty concentrating

Postural Problems

Poor posture from improper seating and screen positioning

Symptoms: Back pain, neck strain, rounded shoulders

Vulnerable Groups

Children

Developing bodies need frequent breaks

Elderly

Vision changes, arthritis risk

Pregnant Women

Postural changes, circulation issues

People with Disabilities

Special equipment may be needed

Ergonomics and Prevention

What is Computer Ergonomics?

Computer ergonomics is a field of study that attempts to reduce strain, fatigue, and injuries by improving the design of a computer and the workspace arrangement.

The 20-20-20 Rule

Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds

This gives your eye muscles a chance to relax and reduces eye strain

Ergonomic Workspace Setup

Monitor: Top of screen at or slightly below eye level, arm's length away (50-70 cm)
Keyboard: Wrists straight, elbows at 90°, keyboard slightly tilted away
Mouse: Close to keyboard, use whole arm movement, not just wrist
Chair: Feet flat on floor, back supported, thighs parallel to floor
Lighting: Reduce glare, use blinds on windows, position monitor perpendicular to windows
Breaks: Take 5-minute breaks every hour, stretch regularly

Quick Desk Exercises

Wrist Stretch

Extend arm, gently pull fingers back. Hold 15 seconds.

Shoulder Rolls

Roll shoulders forward 5x, backward 5x. Releases tension.

Neck Stretch

Slowly tilt head side to side. Hold each side 10 seconds.

Finger Stretch

Spread fingers wide, hold 5 seconds, relax.

Eye Palming

Rub hands together, cup over closed eyes. Relax for 30 seconds.

Stand & Walk

Stand up, walk around for 2-3 minutes every hour.

Computer Lab Safety Rules

Essential Computer Lab Rules

  • No food or drinks near computers (risk of spills and crumbs)
  • Wash hands before using equipment (prevents dirt and oil buildup)
  • Report problems immediately to the teacher or lab technician
  • Follow proper shutdown procedures - don't just turn off power
  • Keep the lab clean and organized - push in chairs, don't leave papers
  • No running or rough play - equipment is expensive and fragile
  • Handle cables carefully - don't pull or yank, don't overload outlets
  • Don't change settings - don't modify system configurations without permission
  • Use antivirus software and don't disable security features
  • No unauthorized software installation - ask permission first

Electrical Safety

  • Use surge protectors to protect against power spikes
  • Don't overload electrical outlets with too many devices
  • Check cables for fraying or damage - report any issues
  • Keep liquids away from electrical equipment
  • Don't use equipment with wet hands
  • During storms, unplug sensitive equipment if possible
  • Use UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) for critical systems

Raising Awareness

Raising awareness about computer health and safety is essential for preventing problems before they occur. Effective publicity campaigns can reach diverse audiences and promote healthy computing habits.
