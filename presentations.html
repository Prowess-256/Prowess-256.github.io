Introduction to Electronic Presentations

Electronic presentations have become the standard for sharing information in business, education, and professional settings. When done well, they can enhance understanding, engage audiences, and make complex information accessible.

Why Presentation Skills Matter

Effective presentations combine visual design, content organization, and delivery skills. Research shows that people remember only 10% of what they hear, but 65% of what they see and hear together.

Presentation Planning & Structure

Before you open your presentation software, planning is essential. A well-structured presentation follows a logical flow that guides your audience through your message.

The 10/20/30 Rule

10 Slides: Maximum number of slides for a 20-minute presentation
20 Minutes: Ideal presentation length to maintain attention
30-Point Font: Minimum font size for readability

Presentation Structure

  1. Title Slide - Clear title, subtitle, presenter name
  2. Agenda/Overview - Outline of presentation structure
  3. Introduction - Hook, problem statement, context
  4. Body (3-5 slides) - Main points with supporting evidence
  5. Case Study/Example - Real-world application
  6. Conclusion - Summary and key takeaways
  7. Q&A Slide - Questions and contact information

Slide Design Principles

Good design makes your presentation more effective, professional, and memorable. Follow these principles to create visually appealing slides.

Common Design Mistakes to Avoid

  • Text-heavy slides: Avoid paragraphs; use bullet points with 5-7 words each
  • Inconsistent formatting: Maintain consistent fonts, colors, and spacing
  • Poor color contrast: Ensure text is readable against background
  • Overuse of animations: Use animations purposefully, not excessively
  • Low-quality images: Use high-resolution, relevant images

Activity: Design a Visual Slide

Objective: Apply design principles to create an effective content slide.

  1. Choose one of your content slides from Activity 1
  2. Create a new slide with a clear, descriptive title
  3. Convert any paragraphs into 3-5 bullet points (max 7 words each)
  4. Choose a professional color scheme (2-3 colors max)
  5. Add a relevant visual element (icon, simple graphic, or image)
  6. Ensure ample white space between elements

Multimedia Elements

Multimedia elements like images, videos, and audio can enhance your presentation when used appropriately. They should support your message, not distract from it.

Using Images Effectively

Image Best Practices

  • Use high-resolution images (avoid pixelation)
  • Choose relevant images that support your message
  • Avoid clipart and overused stock photos
  • Consider using your own photos for authenticity
  • Always cite image sources when required

Incorporating Video and Audio

Best Practices for Video

  • Keep it short: Videos should be 30-60 seconds maximum in presentations
  • Test playback: Always test video playback on the presentation computer
  • Have a backup: Prepare screenshots in case of technical issues
  • Embed properly: Either embed the video or link with a thumbnail

Animation and Transitions

Use animations and transitions purposefully to guide attention, not to entertain.

Animation Type Appropriate Use When to Avoid
Appear/Fade Revealing points one at a time On every element; becomes distracting
Motion Paths Showing processes or movement On text or basic elements
Slide Transitions Signaling section changes Different transitions on every slide
Grow/Shrink Emphasizing key points Overusing on every bullet

Presentation Delivery Techniques

A beautifully designed presentation can still fail if delivered poorly. Effective delivery combines preparation, practice, and engagement techniques.

Know Your Audience

  • Technical level: Adjust terminology based on audience expertise
  • Size: Adjust volume and engagement techniques for audience size
  • Expectations: Understand what your audience hopes to gain
  • Environment: Check room setup, technology, and acoustics in advance

Delivery Best Practices

Speaker Notes View

Key points to cover on each slide

  • Explain the three main benefits
  • Share the case study example
  • Transition to implementation steps

Speaker notes are visible to you, not the audience

Handling Q&A Sessions

  • Prepare anticipated questions: Have slides ready for likely questions
  • Repeat questions: Ensure everyone hears before answering
  • Be honest: It's okay to say "I don't know" and offer to follow up
  • Control time: Set expectations for Q&A duration at the beginning
  • End strong: Close with a final summary or call to action
3
Activity: Practice Delivery with Recording

Objective: Improve presentation delivery through practice and self-review.

  1. Complete your presentation from previous activities (minimum 5 slides)
  2. Add speaker notes to each slide with key talking points
  3. Practice delivering your presentation 3 times
  4. Record yourself (use phone or computer camera)
  5. Watch your recording and note verbal fillers, body language, and pacing
