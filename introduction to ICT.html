Meaning of ICT

What is ICT?

ICT stands for Information, Communication, and Technology. It refers to technologies that provide access to information through telecommunications. Hardly a day passes when ICT does not intrude into our lives.

Understanding the Components

ICT can be compared with other systems to help understand its components. Let's compare ICT with a water system:

Water System Component ICT Equivalent Explanation
Taps, water tank, pipes Technology The physical devices and infrastructure
Water Information The data or content being transmitted
Water flowing through pipes Communication The transmission of information

Common ICT Tools

ICT tools are devices or objects used in ICT for data capture, processing, information storage, and communication. They are not one solid thing but rather a collection of several electronic tools.

Basic ICT Tools

Desktop Computer

Complete computer system for various tasks

Television

Receiving and displaying broadcast signals

Telephone

Voice communication over distance

Radio

Receiving audio broadcasts

Applications of ICT

ICT in Our Society

ICT is increasingly used in all areas of our society to collect and communicate information. ICTs are used in homes, schools, hospitals, banking halls, entertainment industry, security, agriculture, and transport.

Application Areas

Home

Mobile phones for communication, digital cameras for security, smart home devices

School

Digital cameras for security, computers with report card systems, projectors for teaching

Hospitals

Computers with medical records systems, diagnostic equipment, patient monitoring systems

Banking

ATMs, Electronic Point of Sales (EPOS), online banking systems

ICT in Business - EPOS

An Electronic Point of Sales (EPOS) is a self-contained, computerized equipment that performs all tasks of a store checkout counter. It allows payments by cash, bank or credit cards, verifies transactions, generates sales receipts, and coordinates inventory data.

Advantages of Using ICTs
  • Ease communication; fast and convenient
  • Cost effective to process data/communicate
  • Bridge cultural/geographical gaps; make the world a global village

Handling and Maintaining ICT Tools

Importance of Maintenance

Taking care of your ICT tools is just as important as taking care of your books. Both internal and external parts of computers and other ICTs need proper care.

Basic Maintenance Tasks

Keep dust away: Dust your computer regularly
Keep food away: Don't eat or drink while working on computer
Use clean and dry hands: Before typing or using mouse
Keep off during storms: Lightning can damage through electrical connections
Stop virus attack: Use antivirus software and get permission before downloading
Handle CDs with care: Hold correctly by edges

Maintenance Tools and Their Functions

Maintenance Tool Function
Dust blower Can be regularly used to blow dust out of computer equipment
Air conditioner Regulates computer lab temperatures
UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) Provides backup power during outages
Surge protector Protects against voltage spikes

ICT Safety Precautions and Security

Safety Precautions

Importance of Safety

As ICT users, it's important to take precautions when using ICT tools to avoid health problems and ensure equipment longevity. Manufacturers provide user manuals for all ICT equipment with safety guidelines.

5
Activity 1.7: ICT Safety Precautions

Instructions: In groups, discuss the "rules that govern the use of the computer laboratory."

Common Computer Lab Rules
  • No food or drinks near computers
  • Wash hands before using equipment
  • Report any equipment problems immediately
  • Follow proper shutdown procedures
  • Keep the lab clean and organized

Security Threats and Measures

Protecting ICT Tools

ICT tools need protection from risks such as theft, viruses, vandalism, and unauthorized access. Security measures include physical security, electronic security, and document/files and network security.

6
Activity 1.8: ICT Tools Security Threats and Measures

Instructions: Walk around school computer installations and identify security concerns and protection measures.

  1. Take a walk around school computer installations and identify security and safety concerns
  2. Identify how computers need to be protected for long-term use
  3. Discuss security measures in line with physical security, electronic security, and document/files and network security
Security Measures Examples
  • Physical security: Burglar proofing, locked doors, security cameras
  • Electronic security: Passwords, encryption, antivirus software
  • Network security: Firewalls, secure connections, access controls

User Manuals

What are User Manuals?

A user guide or user's guide (commonly known as a manual) is a technical communication document intended to give assistance to people using a particular system. It contains instructions on installing, using, or troubleshooting hardware or software products.

7
Activity 1.9: ICT User Manuals

Instructions: Study a user manual for a given ICT tool and summarize its contents and safety precautions.

  1. Form groups and study the provided user manual
  2. Identify the key sections of the manual
  3. Summarize the contents and safety precautions
  4. Prepare and make a presentation to the class

Knowledge Check: ICT Quiz

1. What does ICT stand for?
Correct! ICT stands for Information and Communication Technology.
Incorrect. The correct answer is Information and Communication Technology.
2. Which of these is NOT a common ICT tool?
Correct! A refrigerator is primarily a household appliance, not typically classified as an ICT tool.
Incorrect. Desktop computers, televisions, and mobile phones are all ICT tools, while refrigerators are primarily household appliances.
3. What does EPOS stand for in business ICT?
Correct! EPOS stands for Electronic Point of Sales, which is computerized equipment used at store checkout counters.
Incorrect. EPOS stands for Electronic Point of Sales, which handles transactions, inventory, and sales receipts in businesses.
