About This Course

This course covers three linked topics: information access, information sharing, and information security. The content uses examples and resources that work in Uganda (government agencies, schools, NGOs, and businesses). The goal is to give learners one week of guided activities with practical tasks, readings, and projects.

Structure: seven days of guided learning. Each day has short readings, practical tasks, group activities, and a small quiz or reflection.

7-Day Learning Plan (Overview)

  1. Day 1: What is information? Sources in your community.
  2. Day 2: Getting information: schools, radio, libraries, internet.
  3. Day 3: Sharing information: groups, WhatsApp, radio and community notice boards.
  4. Day 4: Using data: for schools, small businesses, NGOs.
  5. Day 5: Security basics: passwords, scams, mobile money safety.
  6. Day 6: Policies and responsible sharing: IDs, privacy, and public data.
  7. Day 7: Project day: community plan and showcase.

Day 1 — What is information?

Time: ~3 hours. Aim: understand what counts as information and where it comes from locally.

Step 1 (40–60 min): Read & Discuss

Read the short notes below. Then discuss in pairs: what local sources of information do you use?

Short notes

  • Information = facts, news, data, instructions, or stories that help people act.
  • Sources: schools, radio stations, Ministry announcements, community leaders, NGOs, WhatsApp groups, official websites (e.g., Ministry of Education and Sports).

Step 2 (60–90 min): Mapping activity

Task: In small groups, list all information sources in your village or town. Classify each as: reliable, sometimes reliable, or not reliable. Bring 3 examples to present.

Step 3 (30–45 min): Practical task

Visit or call a local information point (school office, radio station, community leader). Ask: How do they share news? How often? Record answers.

Reflection (15 min)

Write one paragraph: Which source helped you most this week and why?

Day 2 — Getting information

Time: ~3 hours. Aim: learn how to access information and tools that help access.

Read (30 min)

Read about key local services: Ministry of Education and Sports (learning resources), National Identification and Registration Authority (NIRA), Uganda Communications Commission (UCC) for connectivity rules, and NITA-U for ICT services.

Watch (30–45 min)

Watch a short video about using the internet safely; discuss what parts apply where you live.

Hands-on (60–90 min)

  • Practice finding a government document: visit the Ministry of Education website and find the latest guidance for schools.
  • Search UBOS (Uganda Bureau of Statistics) for one statistic useful to a small business (e.g., population of a district).

Group task (30 min)

Make a one-page poster (paper or digital) that shows where community members can get reliable information.

Day 3 — Sharing information

Time: ~3 hours. Aim: practice safe, useful sharing for learning and business.

Short reading (20 min)

How people in Uganda share: radio talk-shows, WhatsApp groups, school notice boards, community meetings, and NGO reports.

Practice (60–90 min)

  1. Create clear messages for three audiences: students, farmers, customers (for a small business). Keep messages short and accurate.
  2. Role-play: one person is a radio presenter; others call in with questions.

Ethics & checks (40 min)

Create a checklist to check facts before sharing: source, date, supporting evidence, trusted local authority (e.g., Ministry, UWA for wildlife info).

Day 4 — Using information (Schools, Businesses, NGOs)

Time: ~3 hours. Aim: use information to solve local problems.

Schools (45–60 min)

Activity: teachers collect learning materials (syllabus, sample exams, videos). Plan a week of lessons using open resources and a local example.

Small businesses (45–60 min)

Activity: find market prices, make a simple price tracker using a notebook or spreadsheet. Use UBOS or local market radio reports.

NGOs & larger projects (45–60 min)

Activity: find an NGO report about water or health in your area. Summarize findings and suggest one small project idea using the data.

Day 5 — Security basics

Time: ~3 hours. Aim: learn practical steps to protect people, money and data.

Read & watch (45 min)

Topics: strong passwords, two-step verification, phishing and scam messages, mobile money safety.

Hands-on (60–75 min)

  1. Make a strong password and set up two-step verification on a sample account.
  2. Practice spotting a fake SMS or WhatsApp message — list signs of fraud.

Local action (30–45 min)

Talk to a local shop or school about securing their money and records. Share a simple checklist (see 'Checklists' section below).

Day 6 — Policy, privacy and responsible sharing

Time: ~3 hours. Aim: understand rules and responsibilities for handling people's data.

Read (45 min)

Find and read short summaries: Uganda Data Protection laws, NIRA guidance on ID data, and NITA-U advisories.

Case study (45–60 min)

Study a local case: how a community used data to plan a school or conserve wildlife (use Uganda Wildlife Authority materials). Discuss risks if data were shared carelessly.

Activity (45–60 min)

Create a one-page data-use policy for a small school or business. Include: what data to collect, who can see it, and how long to keep it.

Day 7 — Project day & showcase

Time: ~3 hours. Aim: present a short project that shows learning from the week.

Project ideas

  • A community information poster that lists reliable sources and emergency contacts.
  • A price tracker notebook or simple spreadsheet for a local market.
  • A short report on how a local school can protect student records.

Presentation (60–90 min)

Each group presents their project. Peers ask 2 questions. Vote on the most useful local project.

Next steps (30 min)

Plan how to keep the project alive: who will update it, where it will be stored, and how to share results with the community or local authorities.

For Schools

Suggested week-long lesson plans, printable checklists, and links to Ministry of Education and Sports materials. Ideas:

  • Weekly reading club using radio lessons and local newspapers.
  • Collect local stories and make a class fact sheet (verify with local sources).
  • Protect student records: keep a paper backup and a password-protected spreadsheet.

For Small Businesses

Practical tips:

  • Find customers: use local radio adverts, notice boards, and WhatsApp groups.
  • Record sales: a simple notebook or spreadsheet helps track stock and prices.
  • Protect money: use official mobile money channels, never share PINs, and keep paper receipts.

For Larger Businesses

Focus on policies and systems:

  • Data retention and backups: schedule regular backups and test restores.
  • Staff training: regular sessions on phishing and secure handling of customer data.
  • Work with NITA-U and UCC for guidance on compliance and secure networks.

For NGOs and Community Groups

Use data responsibly to help communities:

  • Collect only what you need; get consent before sharing personal data.
  • Share public data (e.g., service locations, meeting times) widely using radio and community agents.
  • Coordinate with local government offices (district offices, ministry contacts) when possible.

Case Studies & Local Examples

1. NIRA and ID data

NIRA issues national IDs used for many services. Think about how ID data should be protected and who can access it. Discuss when it is okay to share ID information and when not to.

2. Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA)

UWA shares wildlife reports and park information. This data helps tourism businesses and conservation groups. Consider how open data supports local livelihoods while protecting sensitive locations.

3. Ministry of Education and Sports

The ministry provides curricula and learning resources. Teachers can use official materials and adapt them to local contexts while crediting sources.

Checklists & Tools

Simple security checklist

  • Use strong passwords and different passwords for key accounts.
  • Enable two-step verification where available.
  • Do not share PINs or passwords by phone or message.
  • Keep backups of important records (paper and digital).

Fact-check checklist before sharing

  • Where did this information come from?
  • Is there a date and source?
  • Can a trusted local authority confirm it (Ministry, UWA, school)?

Final Notes

This course is a practical, local-first guide. Use the week plan, adapt activities for your community, and share results with local leaders. If you want, I can expand any day into a printable lesson, add classroom handouts, or produce teacher notes.
