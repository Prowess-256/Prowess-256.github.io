Introduction to Desktop Publishing

What is Desktop Publishing?

Desktop Publishing (DTP) is the process of using computer software to create professional-quality documents for print or digital distribution. DTP combines text, graphics, and layout design to produce publications like brochures, flyers, newsletters, magazines, and books.

DTP vs Word Processing

Primary Use Creating and editing text documents Designing complex layouts with graphics

Design Principles

The Building Blocks of Good Design

Professional-looking publications follow these fundamental design principles. Understanding and applying them will dramatically improve your work.

Balance

Distribute elements evenly for visual stability. Symmetrical or asymmetrical balance.

Contrast

Use differences in size, color, shape, and weight to create visual interest and emphasis.

Alignment

Line up elements to create a clean, organized, and professional look.

Proximity

Group related items together to show relationships and organize information.

Hierarchy

Guide the reader's eye through content by importance using size, color, and placement.

White Space

Use empty space to improve readability and focus attention on important elements.

Repetition

Repeat visual elements (colors, fonts, shapes) to create consistency and unity.

Movement

Direct the viewer's eye through the layout using lines, shapes, and positioning.

Color in Desktop Publishing

Color Modes

  • CMYK (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Black) - Used for PRINT. Colors are created by mixing inks.
  • RGB (Red, Green, Blue) - Used for DIGITAL screens. Colors are created by mixing light.

Preparing for Print

Important Print Terms

Bleed - Extra area beyond the trim line (usually 1/8" or 3mm). Ensures color goes to the edge of the paper.
Trim - The final size of the printed piece after cutting.
Resolution (DPI/PPI) - Dots per inch (print) or Pixels per inch (screen). Print requires 300 DPI minimum.
Margins / Safe Zone - Area where important content should stay (usually 1/4" or 6mm from edge).
Crop Marks - Small lines indicating where to cut the paper.

Print Checklist

  • Convert colors to CMYK (not RGB) for print
  • Ensure images are 300 DPI minimum
  • Add 1/8" bleed if design goes to edge
  • Keep important text 1/4" from edges
  • Embed all fonts or convert to outlines
  • Export as PDF/X-1a or PDF/X-4 (industry standard)
  • Proofread everything before sending to printer
  • Request a physical proof for large print runs

School Event Promotion Project

Your school is hosting an annual cultural festival. You are the lead designer responsible for creating all promotional materials.

FINAL
School Festival Promotional Kit

Requirements:

  1. Brand Identity: Choose a theme, create a color palette (3-5 colors), select 2 fonts (one for headings, one for body)
